# Renault 21 2.0L Purchase & Budget Plan

*Colombia · All figures in COP*

## 1. Overview

This plan covers saving for a used Renault 21 2.0L (target purchase price 5–10 million COP), building a reserve for restoration/upgrades and ongoing running costs, while protecting a fixed leisure allowance and a general long-term savings bucket.

All saved money sits in bank "boxes" earning **9.3% annual interest, paid daily** (compounded). Projections below use 30-day months for simplicity.

## 2. Monthly Income Structure

| Month | Income |

## 3. Budget Buckets & Allocation Rules

Each month's income is allocated in this priority order:

1. **Leisure** — fixed 150,000/month, spent (not saved/compounded).
2. **General savings** — long-term wealth building, not urgent. Schedule: 300,000 in month 1, then 150,000/month until the car-purchase goal (10,000,000) is reached, then it **jumps immediately to 300,000/month** from the following month onward (no ramp).
3. **Purchase fund** — remaining money (income − leisure − savings) is split: **17/21 to the Purchase fund**, **4/21 to the Restoration/running-cost reserve**. This continues until the Purchase fund reaches its 10,000,000 target.
4. **Restoration / running-cost reserve** — once the Purchase fund hits 10,000,000, it is closed (money withdrawn to buy the car, capped at exactly 10,000,000; any surplus beyond that rolls into the Restoration reserve that same month). From that point on, **100% of the monthly remainder** (after leisure + savings) goes into the Restoration reserve.

## 6. Purchase Milestone (Month 6)

- Purchase fund reaches 10,000,000 during month 6.
- Exactly **10,000,000 is withdrawn** to buy the car — no more than this is spent on the purchase itself.
- The surplus above target that month (393,634) is redirected into the Restoration reserve instead of being left idle.
- From month 7 onward, the Purchase fund bucket is closed; all remaining monthly income (after leisure + savings) flows into the Restoration reserve.

## 8. Restoration & Upgrade Plan (Phased)

Priority order: safety first (brakes), then tires (age-related dry rot is a real risk), then suspension, then alignment/balancing (done last so it isn't wasted on a setup about to change).

| Phase | Item | Estimated cost | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Brakes (full front + rear, fluid flush) | 500,000–700,000 | Safety-critical, do first |
| 2 | Tires (set of 4, economy/mid brand) | 800,000–1,400,000 | Confirm stock size before buying |
| 3 | Suspension (front shocks minimum) | 600,000–900,000 | Add rear shocks / bushings if flagged by mechanic |
| 4 | Alignment + balancing | 80,000–150,000 | Do after new tires/suspension are installed |

## 9. Key Assumptions & Caveats

- Projections use 30-day months for simplicity; actual calendar months will shift results by a few days either way.
- Interest assumes the full 9.3%/year daily-compounded rate holds steady; bank rates can change.
- Running-cost and restoration price estimates are general Colombia ballparks, not confirmed local quotes — verify before committing funds.
- The plan assumes no other fixed expenses (rent/food) are drawn from this income; if that changes, percentages should be revisited.
- General savings and leisure are treated as lower priority than the car goals per explicit instruction, with leisure fully protected at 150,000/month throughout.
